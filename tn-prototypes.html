The five questions that change the design

  • Is progress available per chapter, or only per course? Course-only guts C4, C6 and A4.
  • Are assessment scores and free-text answers retrievable? Without them A3 drops from a coaching tool to an attendance report.
  • Can TN provision users, Teams and Content Roles via API? If not, enrolment can't be instant and cohort setup stays manual.
  • Are there outbound webhooks, and for which events? Determines whether the portal feels live or feels like yesterday's data.
  • Does SAML SSO work inside an iframe? If not, C5's embed becomes a new-tab handoff and "seamless" needs redefining.

Candidate portal — module structure

Eight top-level modules plus a Settings group. A module earns its place if it owns a distinct data entity, that entity has its own lifecycle, and a candidate navigates to it deliberately rather than passing through it inside a flow. Anything failing that test is a tab, a panel, or a header element.

Vocabulary — locked

QuizThe free pre-account behavioural questionnaire. Not graded, no pass mark. Produces a bucket and a provisional band.
AssessmentA graded quiz attached to a course chapter. Course-scoped, auto-marked, has a pass mark, attempts and a score. Matches LSVT's own term.
EvaluationThe AI analysis plus reviewer decision from an interview. Produces a level. Not course-linked.
LevelThe resulting band — E1–E5, B1–B5, T1–T5. Owned by the platform, gates course content.

Top level

1 · Overview

Owns nothing of its own — a composite read across every other module.

Why a module the stage-resolution logic is real work: seven candidate states, each with a different next action and a different set of available modules. That decision has to live somewhere, and this is the default route. Renamed from Dashboard because its job is telling someone what to do next, not showing metrics.

2 · My Level

Owns the quiz baseline, every evaluation record, the AI recommendation and reviewer override, the current level, and level history across cycles.

Why a module it owns the single most important entity in the platform. The level gates course content, pricing and eventually employer visibility. Merging the quiz baseline in here means exactly one place answers "what level am I", with the quiz demoted to context inside it.

3 · Interviews

Owns the agent catalogue, availability, bookings, recordings, transcripts, and reschedule / cancel / refund state.

Why separate from My Level a booking has its own lifecycle that runs independently — booked, attended, no-showed, cancelled, refunded. A cancelled interview produces no evaluation at all. It also has browse-and-filter marketplace semantics that My Level has no use for.

4 · Coursework

Owns the enrolment record, chapter structure, video watch state, chapter assessments — attempts, scores, revolutions — and private notes.

Why a module largest data surface in the portal and the daily destination for ninety days. Plural-friendly name because a candidate accumulates several across cycles, and it covers both video and assessments rather than one artefact.

5 · Assignments

Owns weekly task definitions, candidate submissions, due dates, late flags, and the agent's review state.

Why separate from Coursework different data model — free-text submission with a human review loop, on a time-boxed weekly cadence, versus self-paced video and auto-marked assessments. Candidates also need "what's due this week" without navigating course structure. Distinct entity in LSVT too, which helps if you sync it later.

6 · Transcript

Owns no primary data — the permanent record layer over Coursework, Assignments and attendance, plus completion summaries and certificates.

Why a module read pattern. Coursework is current and actionable; Transcript is historical, cross-cycle and exportable. This is what a candidate takes into a re-interview and eventually shows an employer, so it must survive a course closing. Weakest module on the list — defensible as a tab inside Coursework at MVP, as long as it stops being course-scoped once cycle two exists. Must group by course and cycle, or a T4 score sits next to a T3 one with no context.

7 · Cohort

Owns cohort membership, call schedule, attendance, recordings, group discussion, peer standings.

Why a module a cohort is a real entity with its own roster and lifecycle, and this module is scheduled and social rather than content-based. Points and leaderboard live here as a panel, not their own module — they're a view over cohort plus progress.

8 · Messages

Owns one-to-one threads with the assigned agent, with unread state.

Why not folded into Cohort agent messages are private and persist across cohorts and cycles; cohort discussion is group-scoped and dies when the cohort closes. Different retention, different permissions.

Settings group

Payments

Owns transactions, receipts, refunds, payment-provider references. Renamed from Billing because nothing recurs in this model and "Billing" implies a subscription. Must be reachable regardless of course state, and financial records carry retention requirements the rest of the portal doesn't.

Account

Owns identity, contact details, notification preferences, timezone, locale, deletion, and the origin / attribution captured at onboarding. That attribution matters for funnel reporting and has nowhere else sensible to live.

Deliberately not modules

Resources → a tab inside Coursework. Files are always chapter-scoped, so it's contextual navigation, not a destination.

Notifications → header element. Transient and spans every module.

Completion summary and certificate → artefacts inside Transcript, surfaced on Overview when fresh.

Re-interview → not a module. A second booking inside Interviews.

Employer visibility → the obvious eleventh module, left as a gap because your flow diagram marks the scope as unagreed.

Stage gatingThe sidebar is not a fixed list. Coursework, Assignments and Cohort do not exist before enrolment; My Level is thin before the first evaluation. Expand the Settings group by default for nobody — expand nothing, and let Overview do the routing.
